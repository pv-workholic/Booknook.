About Me

A reader trying to become a writer.

Hey There, I'm Pranjal.

I recently closed the chapter on my school years and am officially dedicating my time to the two things I love most: reading books and learning how to write them.

My obsession with literature spans across genres. If a book can make me think, cry, laugh, question reality, or keep me awake until 3 AM, it has a place on my shelf.

I started Book Nook as a literary journal — a place to document my reading journey, collect unforgettable quotes, leave behind midnight annotations, and celebrate stories that linger long after the final page.

Thank you for stopping by.

Happy Reading 🤎

— Pranjal
Books That Stayed

The Shelf That Changed Me

Five books that shaped the way I read, think, and understand stories.

Gone Girl

The book that taught me how powerful an unreliable narrator can be. Every chapter felt like stepping deeper into a carefully constructed trap.

A Man Called Ove

A reminder that quiet stories can leave the deepest marks. It made me laugh, cry, and appreciate ordinary kindness.

The Monk Who Sold His Ferrari

A book that arrived at exactly the right time. It encouraged me to think about ambition, purpose, and balance.

Pride and Prejudice

Sharp, witty, and endlessly readable. Jane Austen somehow makes observations about people feel timeless.

The Catcher in the Rye

Proof that a character doesn't have to be perfect to feel unforgettable. Messy, frustrating, and deeply human.

A Letter Left Between Pages

Dear Future Writer

When I first fell in love with books, I never imagined I would someday dream of writing one. At first, I simply wanted more stories. More worlds. More characters to carry with me after the final page. But somewhere between underlining favourite passages, annotating margins, and staying awake far later than I should have, a different dream quietly appeared. The dream of creating something that might one day sit on someone else's shelf. Maybe years from now, when you look back on this version of yourself, Book Nook will feel small. But remember: Every library begins with a single book. Every writer begins as a reader. And every story begins with a blank page. Keep reading. Keep writing. Keep believing there is room for your voice among all the others.

— Love, Pranjal
